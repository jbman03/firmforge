How to play

Run the firm for ten years

Quick start for beginners
  1. Pick law firm or agency on the start screen. Any name works.
  2. Year 0 is setup with nothing to decide. Tap Skip to opening day.
  3. Tap Next day. Each tap is one workday. The Today at the firm box says what got done.
  4. When a card pops up, read it and pick an answer. Recommended is the by-the-book choice. The game waits until you answer.
  5. Watch Cash and Rule breaks at the top. Red means trouble.
  6. Short on time? Skip to next situation jumps to the next card. Menu then Finish all 10 years shows the final verdict.

The goal. Build a collections business that stays inside the law, keeps its people from burning out, and pays back its money by Year 10 without running out of funding. At the end you get a verdict: GO, GO WITH CONDITIONS, or NO-GO.

1. Pick your path

Name yourself and your firm (or press Random name). The start menu offers a law firm (you are the attorney, owner and CEO) or a licensed collection agency (you are a non-lawyer owner and CEO and suits go to outside law firms). Then choose who does the work: people, or AI agents with a small team. New game brings the menu back at any time.

2. Move time
  • Next day (or the space bar) works one day at the firm. The Today at the firm strip under the top bar says what got done: accounts placed, letters mailed, calls made, money in.
  • Most days the work just gets done. On some days, never on a schedule, something comes up and a card asks what you want to do. Some weeks are quiet and some are slammed, like a real firm. Weekends are closed.
  • Keys: space is Next day, S skips ahead, P starts or stops autoplay.
  • Each new year opens with a Year in review card: money, rule breaks, how you handled situations and who quit.
  • Skip to next situation fast-forwards until something needs you. In Year 0 the same button reads Skip to opening day, because formation has nothing to decide.
  • Autoplay runs time by itself: Play and Pause, with Day to Year setting the size of each jump. Finish all 10 years plays the rest for you and picks the recommended choice on every card.
  • The stamp at the top is the firm's calendar. Year 0 is formation, the first client arrives in Year 1, and suits open in Year 2.
3. Make decisions
  • Time stops when a card appears. Decisions are strategy (hiring, clients, fees, the litigation gate). Manager moments are people and situations: a rep on the phone, a burned-out employee, a resignation.
  • About twice a month on average, but unevenly, a situation lands on a real account, such as a dispute, a bankruptcy or a consumer in distress. Follow the playbook or keep working the file; the rules decide whether that was legal.
  • Month-end rush: the last five business days of every month carry extra dispute, ID-theft and mail work, and more dispute and fraud situations come up. If the desk is near capacity, a card asks whether to approve overtime, borrow account reps or ride it out. Late packets show up as rule breaks.
  • The Recommended option is the compliant one. You can pick anything; bad calls show up as rule breaks.
4. Read the screen
  • Gauges: cash, operating result, staff, new accounts, compliance risk, rule breaks and blind spots.
  • Docket feed: everything that happens. Filter it with the chips. Click an account ID to open its file.
  • Inject a situation (above the feed): drop any situation onto a live account yourself, handled by the book or with the rep going off script.
  • Cash position: your cumulative cash against the plan as written and break-even.
  • Team workload: over 100% means deadlines slip and stress rises. Click a team to see its people.
5. The tabs
  • Go / no-go report: the six tests, every rule break and blind spot with its fix, and how the law firm, agency and AI setups compare.
  • Test cases: every account and what happened to it. Staffing: people needed by team and year.
  • Assumptions: every input with its source (v4.1, law, pilot or guess). Change any of them and start a new run.
  • New run settings: structure, staffing, fees, funding, an attorney partner, and the number of surprise situations.

Everything here is fictional. Legal points are summaries marked [Verify] for attorney review.
